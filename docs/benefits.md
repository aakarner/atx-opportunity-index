## Measures and geography

Each selected type contributes one point if the modeled nearest destination is reachable within the budget, inclusive. A modeled non-reach contributes zero under the 60-minute driving and 90-minute transit model limits. An unknown input makes the corresponding score unknown. A modeled zero-minute trip can occur within the origin cell. The release's complete-basket mean and all eight-type threshold counts at 15, 30 and 60 minutes are reconstructed from the component times and checked on every included H8. The sum describes separate trips. A secondary mean gives the average of the nearest trip times within a profile only when every selected type has a modeled time. Jobs, alternative destination choices, and park acres retain their units. The older-adult and single-adult preliminary baskets currently coincide. The permanent supportive housing basket is partial. Services in the building itself require a project-specific assessment.

For city-clipped tracts, residents from the DAM H8 demographic estimates are apportioned by tract overlap as a fraction of full H8 area. Each score is averaged over the positive, known resident weight in the overlap. This assumes uniform distribution within each H8 cell. Zero-population and unknown-population cells still receive site scores. The tract layer reports the represented resident weight, geometric coverage and area with known weights. Zero represented residents leaves the score unavailable. Per-type tract values are resident-weighted shares meeting the budget. DAM job counts use the existing H8 resident-worker weights with overlap-area fallback where the worker denominator is zero. The earlier 45-minute transit job benchmark retains its original fallback rules.

A map address uses the containing H8. Family school profiles query the current published neighborhood elementary assignment at the point itself, across Austin, Round Rock, Pflugerville, Del Valle and Manor ISDs. Middle and high school assignments are excluded. Campus scores and exclusion reasons come from the existing assigned-school growth pipeline. Overlaps and areas outside available zones are flagged. A tract has no single assigned campus. Travel time to the assigned campus is unmeasured.

The GeoPackage H8 layer retains the source series values named in the dictionary. Columns such as `walk_transit_30_families` count reachable types at H8 and give their resident-weighted mean at tract level. `walk_transit_30_grocery` is a 0/1 at H8 and a 0–1 share at tract level. Nearest-destination status columns preserve the distinction between modeled, unreachable and missing. In the sensitivity table, zero and full count H8 origins. Population-context percentages use the covered full cells, with separate weights for persons, households or families. These are descriptive comparisons for the prototype footprint rather than city population totals.

It compares overall and economically disadvantaged pooled growth at the assigned elementary school only, across Austin, Round Rock, Pflugerville, Del Valle and Manor. A score requires a recognized elementary assignment and available growth measure. The H8 summaries use routing origins, while the three example sites use their actual coordinates.

Rounded subgroup growth is compared with the published whole-point overall measure. Raw values and fixed elementary-campus percentile sensitivities are exported. The reference pools zoned elementary campuses across all five districts with both measures available, one vote per campus. School sample sensitivity uses the minimum of the reading and math growth denominators at the assigned elementary school. It does not depend on achievement-test samples. The comparison reports coverage separately and requires both growth measures when comparing rankings. H8 origin counts are not population counts or independent campus observations.

Employment points follow `min(reachable_jobs / full_credit_target, 1)`. Full transit credit occurs at 50,000 total jobs in 45 minutes. Unlike the earlier binary map flag, the linear rule includes exactly 50,000. Driving full credit is 250,000 jobs within 15 minutes, with 1,000,000 jobs within 30 minutes retained as a sensitivity. Floors of 50%, 60% and 70% are sensitivity scenarios. Education now uses full credit at growth 70 and a raw-growth minimum of 50. The weighted score and fixed minimum status are displayed separately.

Confirmed weights are equal thirds for families, 75% destinations and 25% jobs for older adults, and 40% destinations and 60% jobs for single adults. Adult PSH uses 75% destinations and 25% jobs; 50/50 is retained only as a sensitivity. Education has zero weight for the adult-only presets. Adjustable weights should be normalized to 100% with a visible custom label and reset control. The applicable domains for minimum requirements should be defined by housing profile separately from their adjustable weights. Missing required education must leave the overall assessment incomplete.

Coverage exports now include total residents and children under 18, using the same ACS 2020–2024 block-group estimates and census-block-to-H8 weights. Reconstructed total population must match the existing DAM allocation before child counts are used. Both the `austin` and `metro` crosswalk partitions are required. Missing population is retained as unknown. Geographic assignment, usable overall growth, and subgroup sample thresholds have separate coverage numerators; these are origin-classified whole-hex estimates, not exact boundary-clipped counts. ## Elementary education benchmark decision (November 9, 2026)

Economically disadvantaged growth is the selected education measure. Keep published scores with a flag when either reading or math has fewer than 20 evaluated tests; do not exclude these scores. Overall growth remains contextual information. The adopted full-credit growth benchmark is 70, with a raw-growth minimum of 50. Exactly 50 passes; small-sample flags do not remove or reduce published scores.

Unknown population weights are excluded, not filled with zero.

Median resident-weighted access is 226,810 total jobs in 15 minutes and 970,853 in 30 minutes. Full credit at 250,000 jobs in 15 minutes covers locations representing 45.1% of known residents; 1,000,000 jobs in 30 minutes covers 43.5%. Under linear points, the corresponding resident-weighted means are 69.8 and 85.9 points, so similar full-credit shares do not make the alternatives equivalent. The 15-minute measure emphasizes nearby employment, while 30 minutes represents a broader driving labor market. The user adopted 15 minutes / 250,000 jobs as the default and 30 minutes / 1,000,000 as the sensitivity. The adopted job-domain minimum is 50% of the relevant full-credit benchmark.

Job minimum sensitivity tables now compare 20%, 30%, 40%, and 50% of each mode/time-specific full-credit benchmark, using the same known-resident denominator. The user adopted 50%: 125,000 jobs within 15 minutes driving, 25,000 within 45 minutes transit, and 500,000 within 30 minutes in the driving sensitivity. A 40% floor is a less restrictive alternative. Equality passes. A failed minimum cannot be compensated by other domains; points remain linear and are not zeroed. The sensitivity is a separate assessment, not an override for the primary result. These floors are provisional policy choices, not evidence of adequate job availability or employment outcomes.

## Combined benefits prototype implementation

The public map defaults to hexagons and the family combined-benefits assessment. Driving and transit remain separate selections. All four profiles use fixed required domains: families require destinations, jobs, and education; older, single, and adult PSH require destinations and jobs. Custom nonnegative weights normalize over applicable domains. Zero weight does not waive a minimum or allow a missing required input. All-zero weights are invalid and withhold the score. Presets can be restored with Reset.

A complete location receives the weighted 0–100 score plus either `meets` or `below` minimum status. Missing required input produces `incomplete` and no score, even where another known domain fails; known failures are still displayed. The score is not zeroed when a minimum fails. Small-school-sample flags do not change education points. Numeric classification uses unrounded values. The map offers a continuous score with orange outlines for below-minimum hexagons, plus a separate categorical minimum-status view. Environmental burdens and the earlier jobs-plus-air flag view remain separate from this score.

`R/combined_benefits.R` exports `site/assets/combined-benefits.json`, including per-origin inputs, resident-overlap weights, and independent R results for all four presets and three travel scenarios. `site/combined-benefits.mjs` computes interactive scores and tract summaries. The job sensitivity changes only driving jobs to 30 minutes / 1 million full credit / 500,000 minimum; destinations retain 15 minutes. Destination explorer controls do not change the combined assessment budgets. General/all-eight exploration does not define an additional combined-score preset.

Tract means average complete origin scores using known resident weights multiplied by within-tract hex overlap fractions. Their shares meeting, below, and incomplete use all known overlap-resident weights; missing weights and uncovered area are excluded and disclosed through coverage. A tract mean is never tested against component minimums. Zero known resident weight yields unavailable summaries. Map education uses the hex routing origin; a selected address or map point uses its exact assigned school, so the profile can differ from its hexagon near attendance boundaries.

Validation: browser scoring is compared to R for all 12,348 origin/profile/scenario combinations. Tests cover threshold equality, grocery requirements, zero weights, missing inputs, point school assignments, retained small samples, non-compensable minimums, and tract denominator accounting. Desktop/mobile interaction checks exercise all four presets, custom weights/reset, sensitivity, missingness, and tract summaries.

    About this prototype

    Combined benefits use everyday destinations, job access, and assigned elementary-school growth for family profiles. The weighted 0–100 score is shown separately from minimum status: meets all minimums, below minimum, or incomplete. A strong score cannot override a failed minimum. These are prototype rules, not adopted City standards.

    Comparing benefits and burdens

    The Combined view groups hexagons or census tracts by their benefit and burden scores. Each breakpoint starts at the median of the available scores for the displayed geography and current settings. A score at or above its breakpoint is high. The legend counts locations with both scores and lists the remaining locations as missing.

    Move either breakpoint on the 0–100 score scale to explore a different split. A custom breakpoint stays fixed when you change settings. “Reset to medians” restores both medians for the current settings. Hexagon groups use benefits at the routing origin. Tract groups use the resident-weighted benefit and burden means, each with its own coverage. The location profile also reports benefit minimums, component burden flags and the flood check at the selected point.

    Turn on “Show threshold flags” to outline hexagons with a confirmed benefit minimum miss or at least two high burden components. Select a hexagon to see which rules it triggers. A confirmed flag can appear even when another component is missing. The score sliders change the fill colors independently of these flags.

    Combined benefits rules

    Both destinations and jobs use 15 minutes driving or 45 minutes transit/walking. Jobs earn linear credit up to 250,000 driving or 50,000 transit jobs; their minimum is half the target. The optional 30-minute driving job sensitivity uses a 1-million-job target and 500,000 minimum, while destinations retain 15 minutes. Education uses economically disadvantaged growth, with full credit at 70 and minimum 50; published small-sample scores are flagged and retained.

    Destination full credit requires every basket type. Families and single adults require three of four types; older adults require five of six; adult PSH requires two of three. Older adults and single adults share groceries, pharmacies and urgent care/clinics. Older adults also use senior centers, libraries and parks. Single adults use community/social/recreation centers. Grocery access is required throughout. Family weights are equal thirds. Older adults and adult PSH use 75% destinations / 25% jobs; single adults use 40% / 60%. Adult PSH is provisional and excludes education; family-serving PSH needs an education assessment. The exploratory nine-type basket has no combined score.

    Custom weights are normalized to 100%; they cannot remove required domains or minimums. Missing required input withholds the combined score, even with zero weight. Driving and transit assessments remain separate. Hexagon map scores use school assignments at routing origins; address profiles use the selected point. Tract scores average complete origin scores with resident overlap weights and report the resident shares meeting, falling below, and missing minimum assessments. Unknown population and uncovered areas are excluded and disclosed.

    Benefits: job access

    Job access is scored using the targets and travel budgets above. The transit estimate uses median travel times across departures in the July 13, 2026, 7–8:59 a.m. window, using a June 25, 2026 routing/transit snapshot and 2023 LODES jobs. Wage groups are breakdowns of total jobs, not additional scores.

    Benefits: assigned elementary school

    Family profiles show overall and economically disadvantaged academic growth at the elementary school assigned to the selected point. Austin, Round Rock, Pflugerville, Del Valle, Manor, Eanes, Leander, Hays and Lake Travis are included. Middle and high schools are excluded. The measure uses the districts’ currently published attendance locators for 2026–27 and TEA’s 2025–26 campus outcomes; older source-layer labels remain visible in each profile. Lake Travis uses approximate boundaries extracted from its currently linked georeferenced PDF, labeled effective 2023–24. Hays assignments reflect November 10, 2026; its January 2027 move-in exception is not yet applied. Transfers, specialized programs and school travel time are not scored. Missing scores and conflicting assignments stay unavailable. Coverage is tracked for represented residents and children under 18; child counts are population estimates, not enrollment.

    Combined burdens

    Heat contributes one third of the 0–100 score. Crashes, sidewalk gaps on major streets, industrial air and near-road traffic each contribute one sixth. Higher scores mean greater burden. The combined score requires all five components. Overall high burden requires at least two high components among heat, industrial air, near-road traffic, crashes, and sidewalk gaps on major streets. Classification is independent of the weighted score. Two known high components retain the overall high classification when another component is missing; fewer than two with missing data leave the assessment incomplete. A complete assessment with zero or one high component is below the overall high-burden threshold. Each individual high-component warning remains visible. Secondary industrial review and limited heat observations are separate badges. Turn off “Show hexagons” for census tract estimates. Each tract mean weights overlapping hexagons by their estimated residents. The combined mean uses hexagons with all five scores. Tract profiles show the share of represented residents with a complete score and the share in hexagons classified as high burden.

    Burdens: heat

    Heat uses the relative percentile of 2026 daytime land-surface temperature across study hexagons, with a high-burden flag at the 90th percentile. Each date must observe at least half the hexagon’s land. Fewer than eight eligible dates receive a limited-observation flag; their scores are retained. Hot-pixel share at or above 120°F is context only. Surface temperature is not air temperature.

    Burdens: crashes and sidewalk gaps on major streets

    Crash density and sidewalk gaps on major streets are measured within half-mile circles centered on hexagons and clipped to Austin. Crashes are unique fatal or serious-injury crashes in 2020–2024, annualized per observed square mile. The crash score rises linearly to 100 at 6.112 crashes per year per square mile; high burden starts at 4.277. These frozen local reference values are screening anchors, not safety standards or traveler risk rates.

    The sidewalk gap score is the percentage of major-street length without a mapped sidewalk or parallel path, counting each side separately. A mile of road with a sidewalk on just one side scores 50%, the high-burden cutoff. Local residential streets are outside the measure. The City street network defines eligible roads, and the sidewalk inventory and existing urban trails identify facilities. Areas with no eligible major street, or with neither pedestrian inventory nor mapped coverage, have no score. The quarter-mile sensitivity uses the same rules.

    Burdens: industrial air

    Industrial-air points rise linearly to 100 at RSEI ToxConc 622.7. High burden starts at 351.1; 100 to below 351.1 is a separate secondary-review band. Displayed thresholds are rounded; calculations use exact frozen values. EPA RSEI 2022, model v2.3.12, represents modeled TRI-related releases, not all pollution. This is screening, not a health-risk probability or safe-exposure threshold.

    Burdens: near-road traffic

    The score is the percentage of the full hexagon within 300 meters of roads carrying more than 50,000 vehicles per day. Roads outside Austin contribute. High burden starts at 50% overlap; the optional 25% flag sensitivity changes component flags and overall classification, not scores. Distance from the H8 center to the nearest qualifying road is context, not an address measurement.

    What the geography means

    Tracts are clipped to the municipal boundary, including Dog’s Head. City of Austin full- and limited-purpose limits. The extraterritorial jurisdiction (ETJ) is excluded. Boundary source. Daily destination scores use resident weights within the covered area. RSEI is area weighted from EPA’s native 810 m grid. Tract averages can hide local variation.

    Address searches identify the containing H3 resolution 8 cell. Access is estimated from that cell’s origin, and RSEI is the mean over the full cell. These estimates describe the hexagon containing the address. Available hexagons cover the reported share of the city area. Missing coverage stays unavailable. Tract lookup uses generalized Census boundaries and is approximate near edges.

    Development eligibility: flood constraint

    The City of Austin fully developed floodplain is a separate hard constraint. A full hexagon entirely covered by the mapped floodplain is ineligible for development under this prototype rule. Partial overlap requires an address-level check: a selected point inside or on the boundary is ineligible, while a point outside is not excluded by this flood rule. Missing coverage remains unknown. Select “Show actual floodplain” in any map view to see the footprint. Dark outlines and fill mark fully excluded hexagons. Eligibility cannot be offset by benefit scores, custom weights, or low environmental burdens. This is a project eligibility rule, not a permit determination.

    Reading the flags

    Benefit scores are displayed separately from their required minimums. Burden scores are displayed separately from high-component warnings and data-quality flags. Benefits and burdens remain separate; there is no net benefits-minus-burdens score. Flood eligibility is a separate constraint. Missing values remain unavailable. A low burden score or absence of a warning does not establish safety. Classifications use unrounded values.

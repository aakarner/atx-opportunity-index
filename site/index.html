    About this prototype

    Combined benefits use everyday destinations, job access, and assigned elementary-school growth for family profiles. The weighted 0–100 score is shown separately from minimum status: meets all minimums, below minimum, or incomplete. A strong score cannot override a failed minimum. These are prototype rules, not adopted City standards.

    Combined benefits rules

    Both destinations and jobs use 15 minutes driving or 45 minutes transit/walking. Jobs earn linear credit up to 250,000 driving or 50,000 transit jobs; their minimum is half the target. The optional 30-minute driving job sensitivity uses a 1-million-job target and 500,000 minimum, while destinations retain 15 minutes. Education uses economically disadvantaged growth, with full credit at 70 and minimum 50; published small-sample scores are flagged and retained.

    Destination full credit requires every basket type. Families and single adults require three of four types; older adults require five of six; adult PSH requires two of three. The older-adult basket includes grocery, pharmacy, urgent care, community/senior centers, libraries, and parks. Grocery access is required throughout. Family weights are equal thirds. Older adults and adult PSH use 75% destinations / 25% jobs; single adults use 40% / 60%. Adult PSH is provisional and excludes education; family-serving PSH needs an education assessment. The exploratory eight-type basket has no combined score.

    Custom weights are normalized to 100%; they cannot remove required domains or minimums. Missing required input withholds the combined score, even with zero weight. Driving and transit assessments remain separate. Hexagon map scores use school assignments at routing origins; address profiles use the selected point. Tract scores average complete origin scores with resident overlap weights and report the resident shares meeting, falling below, and missing minimum assessments. Unknown population and uncovered areas are excluded and disclosed.

    Benefits: job access

    Job access is scored using the targets and travel budgets above. The transit estimate uses median travel times across departures in the July 13, 2026, 7–8:59 a.m. window, using a June 25, 2026 routing/transit snapshot and 2023 LODES jobs. Wage groups are breakdowns of total jobs, not additional scores.

    Benefits: assigned elementary school

    Family profiles show overall and economically disadvantaged academic growth at the elementary school assigned to the selected point. Austin, Round Rock, Pflugerville, Del Valle and Manor are included. Middle and high schools are excluded. The measure uses the districts’ currently published attendance locators for 2026–27 and TEA’s 2025–26 campus outcomes; older source-layer labels remain visible in each profile. Transfers, specialized programs and school travel time are not scored. Missing scores and conflicting assignments stay unavailable. Coverage is tracked for represented residents and children under 18; child counts are population estimates, not enrollment.

    Combined burdens

    The 0–100 score gives equal thirds to heat, air, and street safety. Air splits equally between industrial air and near-road traffic (one sixth each); street safety splits equally between crashes and pedestrian gaps. Higher scores mean greater burden. All four domain inputs are required: missing data withhold the combined score, without redistributing weights. Any high component triggers a separate warning, even if the overall score is low or incomplete. Secondary industrial review and limited heat observations are separate badges. Maps use the 1,029 study hexagons; no tract-level composite is inferred.

    Burdens: heat

    Heat uses the relative percentile of 2026 daytime land-surface temperature across study hexagons, with a high-burden flag at the 90th percentile. Each date must observe at least half the hexagon’s land. Fewer than eight eligible dates receive a limited-observation flag; their scores are retained. Hot-pixel share at or above 120°F is context only. Surface temperature is not air temperature.

    Burdens: street safety

    Crash and pedestrian-gap scores receive equal weights within half-mile circles centered on hexagons and clipped to Austin. Crashes are unique fatal or serious-injury crashes in 2020–2024, annualized per observed square mile. The crash score rises linearly to 100 at 6.112 crashes per year per square mile; high burden starts at 4.277. These frozen local reference values are screening anchors, not safety standards or traveler risk rates.

    Pedestrian gaps are the percentage of inventoried existing-plus-planned facility length still planned, excluding potential facilities. High burden begins at 50%. Empty denominators and unclassified inventories remain unavailable; past completion dates on planned facilities are flagged for review. Either crash or gap warning remains visible regardless of the combined street score. The quarter-mile sensitivity retains the same scoring anchors.

    Burdens: industrial air

    Industrial-air points rise linearly to 100 at RSEI ToxConc 622.7. High burden starts at 351.1; 100 to below 351.1 is a separate secondary-review band. Displayed thresholds are rounded; calculations use exact frozen values. EPA RSEI 2022, model v2.3.12, represents modeled TRI-related releases, not all pollution. This is screening, not a health-risk probability or safe-exposure threshold.

    Burdens: near-road traffic

    The score is the percentage of the full hexagon within 300 meters of roads carrying more than 50,000 vehicles per day. Roads outside Austin contribute. High burden starts at 50% overlap; the optional 25% flag sensitivity changes warnings only. Distance from the H8 center to the nearest qualifying road is context, not an address measurement.

    What the geography means

    Tracts are clipped to the municipal boundary, including Dog’s Head. City of Austin full- and limited-purpose limits. The extraterritorial jurisdiction (ETJ) is excluded. Boundary source. Daily destination scores use resident weights within the covered area. RSEI is area weighted from EPA’s native 810 m grid. Tract averages can hide local variation.

    Address searches identify the containing H3 resolution 8 cell. Access is estimated from that cell’s origin, and RSEI is the mean over the full cell. These are neighborhood proxies, not parcel measurements. Available hexagons cover the reported share of the city area. Missing coverage stays unavailable. Tract lookup uses generalized Census boundaries and is approximate near edges.

    Development eligibility: flood constraint

    The City of Austin fully developed floodplain is a separate hard constraint. A full hexagon entirely covered by the mapped floodplain is ineligible for development under this prototype rule. Partial overlap requires an address-level check: a selected point inside or on the boundary is ineligible, while a point outside is not excluded by this flood rule. Missing coverage remains unknown. The Eligibility view shows the actual floodplain footprint within the study hexagons. Toggle “Show actual floodplain” to compare the footprint with whole-hex status colors, or overlay it on another map view. Eligibility cannot be offset by benefit scores, custom weights, or low environmental burdens. This is a project eligibility rule, not a permit determination.

    Power outages are excluded from the scope. Heat, air, and street safety form the three equally weighted themes. Flooding has no weight in that score.

    Reading the flags

    Benefit scores are displayed separately from their required minimums. Burden scores are displayed separately from high-component warnings and data-quality flags. Benefits and burdens remain separate; there is no net benefits-minus-burdens score. Flood eligibility is a separate constraint. Missing values remain unavailable. A low burden score or absence of a warning does not establish safety. Classifications use unrounded values.
